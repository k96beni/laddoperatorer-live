What is depot charging and what does it require?

Depot charging is the charging of a vehicle fleet at the haulier's own site, where vehicles return between shifts (return-to-base) and are charged, usually overnight. It requires sufficient grid connection and power, smart control that distributes power between vehicles, and enough reliability for business-critical traffic. Most depots manage with CCS-DC and control; MCS is mainly needed for long-haul.

Unlike passenger-car charging, depot charging is about many vehicles, short charging windows and high power – where an uncharged truck in the morning means a production stop. Power management (peak shaving), departure-time control and load balancing determine both reliability and cost, since the power tariff is often the largest item.

AC, DC and MCS – in brief

  • AC – slow charging, rarely enough for heavy vehicles at a depot.
  • CCS-DC – 50–400 kW, standard for today's depots with overnight charging.
  • MCS – megawatt charging for long-haul on the road; in serial production from 2026.

TCO – what does depot charging cost per vehicle per year?

The total cost of ownership (TCO) for depot charging is driven mainly by the power tariff, energy, hardware/installation and operation. With peak shaving and spot-price control, energy and power costs can be reduced significantly – in well-configured depots by up to around 12% lower energy cost. Calculate total cost over time, not just charger price.

A text example: a depot that moves charging to the cheap night hours and caps power peaks reduces both variable energy cost and the subscribed power ceiling – often so that a costly grid upgrade can be postponed or avoided. Model your depot in the TCO calculator.

Frequently asked questions about depot charging

Which charge point operator should a haulier choose for depot charging of electric trucks?
Choose a CPO that takes full responsibility: hardware-agnostic operation (OCPP), power and spot-price control, payment flows for fleet and guests, and 24/7 support with an SLA. ChargeNode delivers the whole chain in-house.
What does depot charging cost per truck per year?
It depends on the number of vehicles, battery size, charging windows and electricity contract. The largest items are power and energy – both reduced with peak shaving and spot-price control. Calculate TCO, not just hardware price.
Do I need DC or MCS for depot charging?
Most return-to-base depots manage with CCS-DC and smart control. MCS is mainly needed for fast charging of long-haul trucks. ChargeNode operates CCS-DC today and is MCS-ready via partners.
What is the difference between a charge point operator and a hardware supplier?
A hardware supplier makes the chargers; a charge point operator (CPO) handles operation, control, payment and support. ChargeNode works OCPP-agnostically on top of leading hardware and takes full responsibility.
Can ChargeNode operate hardware we already have?
Yes. ChargeNode is hardware-agnostic and operates chargers that follow OCPP (1.6 or later). You usually do not need to replace hardware to change operating partner.
How does payment work for your own fleet versus external guests?
Own fleet charges via Fleet account or RFID with a consolidated invoice and cost per vehicle. External guests pay via app, card or QR. All in one platform with separate payment flows.
What support and SLA are included?

How do I scale from 10 to 100+ charging points?
